# Timed numbered-ball prototype

The guided entry first shows **NORMAL / ΜΕ BOOST** when the headset is mounted or Desktop Editor Play starts. Touch a button with a tracked catching hand, or click it in the desktop Game view, to start the full eight-stage presentation. Both modes include safety, sequential branding, introduction, the main game and Second Chance. Normal then shows the final score and quiet exit; Με Boost adds the existing 25-second Boost wave after Second Chance and before the finale, provided there are eligible caught numbers. Player restart remains hidden in the guided flow. See [EXPERIENCE_FLOW.md](EXPERIENCE_FLOW.md) for timing, placeholder text, operator hooks and validation. The round details below also describe the underlying standalone mechanics.

Operator APIs are `BeginNormalSession()`, `BeginBoostSession()` and `ShowModeSelection()` on `KinoExperienceController`. Reading screens default to **2.2 × 1.4 m at 2.5 m**, configurable with `contentWidth` and `contentDistance`. The separate touch choice is centered at `modeViewOffset = (0, -0.18, 0.46)` m, with two **0.22 × 0.11 m** buttons separated by **0.04 m**. All are World Space canvases. Source geometry was checked against [Meta's hand UI guidance](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands-ui-best-practices/); actual seated reach, stereo readability and comfort still need testing on Quest.

Branding shows Allwyn for **3 seconds**, black for **0.5 seconds**, then KINO for **3 seconds**, on a black background. Each logo duration includes **0.65-second** fades in and out. `AllwynOnBlack.png` derives from the supplied SVG, preserving its geometry and cyan symbol while using a white wordmark for contrast. The original logo files remain preserved.

Open `Assets/KinoRotunda/Scenes/KinoRotunda.unity` and press Play. This is now the enabled build scene. The original `KINO_VR_Game` test scene is retained.

The `KINO Gameplay` prefab instance contains the round, launcher, live board and a copy of the existing VR rig with both hand catchers. On headset it uses the VR head position. In the Editor without an active XR device it uses a stationary desktop camera for previewing the room and launches.

## Current rules

- Each round launches **20 numbered balls over 60 seconds**, one every 3 seconds at 0, 3, …, 57 seconds. The launcher alternates between the existing tubes. The quota counts successful launches, so a full pool defers a slot without consuming it.
- Caught ordinary balls award **1 point** and highlight their number in yellow. Missed balls never add a board marker or an eligible bonus number.
- A ball touching the floor disappears immediately and counts as missed. Catch/miss guards resolve each ball once. The existing six-second lifetime is a fallback for balls that leave the playable area.
- **2–6 of the 20 numbered balls glow** and give +2 instead of +1. **2–6 extra dark Mystery balls (?)** appear in random internal gaps; catches activate ×2, ×3 or ×4 for `(main duration / 20) × 2` seconds (6 seconds at 60, 9 at 90). Each new Mystery replaces the multiplier and restarts its timer. It affects future numbered catches, expires to ×1 and clears before the next phase. Mystery itself adds no points or board number. Contact popups show +1, +2 or the rolled multiplier at the catch location. See `EXPERIENCE_FLOW.md` for setup, presentation and validation.
- The timer reaches **00:00** at 60 seconds. Any remaining ordinary flight stays catchable until it is caught, touches the floor, or expires; the board shows **LAST BALLS** during this short finish. The final bonus waits until all 20 numbered balls and the extra Mystery balls are resolved.
- If at least one ordinary ball was caught, **one red KINO Bonus** launches after the 20-ball draw, before Second Chance.
- The bonus cycles only through the distinct ordinary numbers actually caught, starting at a random candidate and changing about once per second. **Bonus Number Interval** controls readability. With one eligible number it stays fixed; with no ordinary catches, skip the red bonus and continue to Second Chance.
- Catching the bonus locks its displayed number, awards **3 points**, emits the red catch effect and turns that existing board oval red with a white number. Missing it awards nothing. Either outcome continues to Second Chance; the red bonus never launches twice.
- After the red bonus resolves (or is skipped), keep the board visible for **3 seconds**, fade the entire view to black over **1 second**, then fade back over **1 second** onto the supplied **2η ΕΥΚΑΙΡΙΑ** logo. The announcement stays visible for two more seconds. No balls launch during the transition/title.
- **Second Chance launches exactly 3 additional green balls**, one every 3 seconds. Each catch gives **3 points**, a green catch effect and a green board marker. Earlier red markers retain priority. The score and all previous catches remain. This makes **23 numbered draw balls**, plus 2–6 extra Mystery balls and the separate red bonus. Wait until all green flights resolve before finishing.
- **Showcase Boost After Second Chance** is set per session by the guided mode choice: Normal disables it, Με Boost enables it. In a standalone round it remains an Inspector option. After Second Chance it cuts directly to the existing gold KINO BOOST announcement and room treatment, followed by a **25-second** wave at **one ball per second**, with faster flight. Boost reuses only numbers caught in the normal draw or Second Chance; every catch, including repeated catches of the same number, gives **3 points**, pulses its existing marker and displays **x3** beside it. No new board numbers are introduced. If there are no eligible numbers after Second Chance, finish without a Boost wave. Duration and interval are Inspector settings.
- Normal completes the round after all three green balls resolve; Με Boost waits until its optional Boost wave and remaining flights resolve. The guided controller then shows the finale with the accumulated score and proceeds to the quiet exit.
- The header shows **SCORE** and **CAUGHT nn / 20** for ordinary catches. The timer bar remains full during the final bonus. **ROUND COMPLETE** retains the score and marked numbers, and `onRoundFinished` fires once after the whole sequence.
- With the guided controller disabled, a standalone **RESTART** button appears in front-left of the player only after the whole round finishes (including the optional Boost). Touch it with either catching hand, or click it in the desktop Game view, to start the round loop again with score, board, timer, balls and phase state reset. Its position stays fixed while the player looks around; the Inspector's **View Offset** adjusts placement. It disappears during gameplay. A short 0.35-second guard prevents the final catch from immediately restarting the game. The guided flow instead returns to mode selection after headset removal/replacement or an operator call to `ShowModeSelection()` once complete.
- Ordinary balls still draw random numbers from **1–80**, with repeats allowed. `BeginRound()` resets draw slots, caught-number memory, miss/catch counts, bonus eligibility, timer, board and all flying balls.


The live board keeps the existing KINO artwork and frame. Native UI replaces the static sample numbers and time fields with the live grid and countdown. The original screen material, texture, architecture and lighting are preserved.

The header logo uses the independent `KINO-logo-RGB.png` source through a cropped UV rectangle, instead of the small logo baked into the 1065 × 602 background. Its import uses trilinear Kaiser mipmaps, aniso 8, a modest -0.35 mip bias and Android ASTC 4×4. Mipmaps remain enabled to control shimmer. The original PNG is untouched. This fixes the source-resolution/filtering weakness; head-angle sharpness still needs checking on the headset.

The gold BOOST presentation and voice assets run in the guided Με Boost mode and in standalone rounds with Boost enabled. The red KINO Bonus and gold KINO BOOST are separate phases.

`Textures/SecondChanceLogo.png` is a transparent render of the supplied vector PDF, including its white letter faces. `Tools/KinoSecondChance/extract_logo.py` regenerates it with PyMuPDF, Pillow and NumPy. Its Unity import uses trilinear mipmaps, aniso 8 and Android ASTC 4x4. The blackout uses a stereo-aware world-space canvas following the active desktop/VR view, with a shared overlay shader.

The catchable balls use a smooth horizontal oval mesh (1.68:1, matching the board markers), with uniform transforms so the text stays unstretched. A horizontal capsule collider follows their width and height. The black number remains centred toward the viewer at oblique angles, without a white badge. The physical balls and board tokens share a yellow lacquer palette and softbox/rim highlights through `KinoGoldSurface.hlsl`.

The live display adds slowly travelling cyan light around the number frame, soft background glows and a drifting blue ribbon, inspired by the reference video. Animation runs in the shared UI shader without additional scene lights or per-frame material allocation. The four `Board*` background materials expose **Neon motion speed** and **Neon brightness**; set motion speed to zero for a still background.

## Background music

`KinoAudioController` loops the supplied ChilloutMusic, including between standalone rounds and across restarts, and crossfades to a separate baked instrumental during Boost. The guided controller keeps session audio silent during mode selection and startup, brings it in with the introduction and fades it out during closing. Adjust Music, Effects, Ambience and Announcement on the controller (defaults 0.25 / 0.8 / 0.3 / 0.8). The existing BOOST announcement remains separate and ducks the music. The controller owns playback, so its sources have Play On Awake disabled.

`Audio/ChilloutMusicLoop.wav` is derived from the supplied `ChilloutMusic.mp3` (44.1 kHz stereo). The source file is unchanged. The 2.27-second silent tail is removed at 66.460907 seconds, and the last two seconds blend linearly with the first two seconds before wrapping. A 3 dB gain reduction leaves headroom when decoding the MP3 to PCM. The resulting loop is 64.460907 seconds; it imports as streaming Vorbis at quality 0.7. Check final loudness and the loop transition on the headset.

The first-pass bank includes baked tube rise/exit/approach effects, four catch families, floor taps, phase announcements, final/restart/countdown cues, courtyard breeze, stylized birds, a lottery motor and Boost music. `Tools > KINO VR > Audio` exposes setup, clip replacement, mix controls and playback/sequence validation. All generation happens offline in `Tools/KinoAudio/bake_audio.py`; no audio is synthesized at startup or during Play. See `Tools/KinoAudio/README.md` for the cue list, listening reel, replacement workflow and performance checks.

## Tracked hand appearance

The two catching hands in `KinoTimedGameplay.prefab` share `Materials/HandSatin.mat`, using `KINO/Hand Satin`: midnight blue satin, a broad cool highlight, champagne gold reflections and a restrained cyan edge. The palette follows the Rotunda's blue marble/brass and the Allwyn/display cyan. The opaque surface retains finger depth and silhouette without a transparent overlay. Room probes influence the body lightly, while the stylized highlights keep hands readable over the black instruction screens. The existing instruction-screen helper preserves this material while changing its draw queue, then restores it for gameplay; Meta continues to control tracking visibility and its system-gesture material.

Adjust **Midnight blue satin**, **Gold reflection strength** and **Cyan edge brightness** on `HandSatin.mat`. **Edge breathing amount** defaults to only 4.5%; set it or **Edge breathing cycles per second** to zero for a still finish. The shader has no textures, cubemap sampling or additional realtime lights, with one colour pass and optional URP depth/normal passes. It includes stereo instancing support; headset appearance and performance still need Quest verification.

`Tools > KINO VR > Hands` applies the material, validates both hand assignments and the instruction/gameplay material restoration, and captures previews using Meta's OpenXR reference hand meshes. Reports and renders are saved under `Artifacts/KinoGameplay/Hands`. The gameplay setup also applies this hand style when regenerating the rig. Preview meshes are temporary and are never saved into the scene.

## Air in the tubes and lottery

The saved Rotunda scene contains **KINO air balls**: nine numbered ovals in each of the five decorative tubes and fourteen in the glass lottery beneath the screen. `DecorativeNumberedBall.prefab` is a variant of the gameplay ball, inheriting its yellow lacquer and number-facing transform. It overrides the mesh with `Meshes/KinoDecorativeOvalBall.asset`: 24 × 12 segments, 325 vertices and 576 triangles, with the same 1.68:1 dimensions, pivot, analytic normals and UV layout. The catchable gameplay ball uses `Meshes/KinoOvalBall.asset` at 32 × 16 segments, 561 vertices and 1,024 triangles. Both meshes have fixed detail, with no LOD: the player is stationary and cannot approach the tubes or lottery. Tube balls retain gameplay scale; lottery balls use a smaller uniform scale to fit the existing dome. Text remains unstretched and follows the same player-facing surface placement as gameplay.

**Tools > KINO VR > Air balls > 4 - Apply ball mesh budgets** updates the existing gameplay mesh and decorative prefab without rebuilding the scene or changing physics. The gameplay and air-ball setup generators preserve their respective mesh budgets. Menu 2 checks both meshes, matching shape/number placement, and all 59 decorative scene references.

Decorative numbers use the existing Liberation Sans SDF atlas through a shared material. `KinoNumberGeometry.asset` stores TMP-generated glyph geometry for 1–80, including the original spacing, bold weight and signed SDF scale. Each chamber's `KinoNumberBatch` updates its number vertices after ball motion, with separate front/back meshes to retain sorting around the transparent glass. There are no decorative canvases or individual `KinoBallNumber.LateUpdate` callbacks; the disabled number components retain their references and expose `Number` for validation. Gameplay balls retain their TMP labels. **Air balls > 5 - Batch decorative numbers** regenerates the geometry and upgrades the decorative/air prefabs. The normal air-ball setup also creates this configuration.

`KinoAirChamber` applies pulsing lift, falling motion and small eddies to the tube balls. The five decorative tubes keep the same continuous idle motion during gameplay, between rounds and during countdowns; round transitions do not pause or reset their air simulation. The guided controller waits for a mode choice, then calls `BeginRound()` only after startup, safety, branding and introduction complete. Tube 26 and Tube 02 are reserved for gameplay and contain no decorative balls. The launcher alternates between their existing `SpawnPosition` markers, rises inside each shaft, then clears `ExitPosition` above the rim before smooth airflow and turbulence carry the ball towards a sampled hand-height target. The three old front launch points have been removed.

Lottery balls circulate around the existing spindle with bounded contacts and tumble inside the dome. **Idle Mix Speed** defaults to 0.32, **Gameplay Mix Speed** to 1.65, with a smooth 0.8-second speed change. **Tube Air Strength** adjusts the lift. The chamber Inspector exposes these controls independently. The simulation uses fixed steps and local deterministic phases, without consuming gameplay randomness, adding rigidbodies, or awarding catches.

The old 77 imported ball renderers are disabled in the scene; their FBX source remains available for regeneration. `KinoAirBalls.prefab` stores the reusable arrangement. After rebuilding the environment, use **Tools > KINO VR > Air balls > 1 - Set up tube and lottery balls** again. Menu 2 validates shapes, contacts, containment and transitions; menu 3 captures previews. `KinoVR.Editor.KinoAirChamberSetup.BatchRun` also reloads the scene and checks the round lifecycle, actual spawning/catching and continuous decorative motion in Play Mode. Reports and motion frames are under `Artifacts/KinoGameplay/AirBalls`. Headset appearance and GPU cost need validation on the target device.

Gameplay balls are prewarmed once (16 objects, including TMP buffers). Catch, expiry, round restart and launcher disable return each object to the pool. Every lease resets catch state, number/controller/viewer, physics, rotation and expiry; a generation guard rejects stale returns. A full pool defers a scheduled slot without stealing an active ball or reducing the 20-ball quota. Use **Tools > KINO VR > 8 - Select flight difficulty** to select the launcher. Under **Difficulty**, **Turbulence** sets sideways/vertical deviation (0–2 metres, default 0.35), **Turbulence Frequency** sets the rate of those changes (default 1), **Exit Acceleration** sets the wind acceleration limit (default 28 m/s²), and **Flight Time** sets exit-to-player duration for new balls (default 2.6 seconds; lower is faster). Turbulence and acceleration can be adjusted during Play. Stop Play and set the chosen values in Edit mode to keep them. Under **Orientation**, **Wobble Angle** defaults to 3 degrees; the oval stays horizontal and turns towards the player, while its number keeps facing the viewer. Rotational physics cannot make it tumble, even with stronger turbulence. **Tools > KINO VR > 6 - Tube airflow and gameplay pool** reapplies the setup; menu 7 tests both trajectories, pool exhaustion/reuse, stale deadlines, restart and clearing. Runtime flight remains physically catchable after it clears the tube.
